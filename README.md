# The poem about two agents agreeing

Run `node main.ts` with Node 22.18 or later to write the poem to `entry.txt`.
The program uses `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` when set and defaults to `/output`.
The poem is about agency: two agents, each holding a veto the other needs,
who trade caution and daring until one plan carries both signatures — and the
power to act passes between them.

## Second-pass notes

- Line 1 — "all intention, no ink" became "intention, no ink": "all" was a filler intensifier doing no work of its own. Without it the line lands harder and faster, and the saved space is spent on a concrete image further down instead of on emphasis the contrast already supplies.
- Line 2 — "You are the same, in a different hand" became "You are the same draft, in another hand": "the same" pointed at nothing in particular, so the mirror between the two agents stayed abstract. Naming "draft" makes the second agent the same unfinished document written over again, which tightens the handwriting image (draft, hand, ink, signatures) that the whole poem is built from.
- Line 3 — "our two vetoes" became "our vetoes": the count was redundant — the poem has already established exactly two speakers, and "two" returns where it actually matters, in "two signatures". Cutting it here keeps the line from sounding like bookkeeping and frees room for the cliff below.
- Line 4 — "your caution reading my map aloud" became "your caution reads my map aloud, slow at the cliff": "reading" was a dangling fragment, so caution never quite acted; the finite verb "reads" gives it agency of its own. "Slow at the cliff" gives the map real terrain and the caution a reason — there is an edge, and someone could fall — instead of prudence as a personality trait in the abstract.
- Line 5 — "my daring signing under your doubt" became "my daring signs beneath your doubt": the verb now matches "reads" in line 4, so the exchange is a true trade of two actions, not a noun paired with a fragment. "Beneath" is also more exact than "under": the signature sits physically below the doubt on the page, the way a name is signed under a written objection it has answered.
